How I Use Notion as a Freelance Brand Designer

Notion transformed how I organize my freelance brand design business. It replaced scattered documents, unread client emails, and lost inspiration with a single, adaptable workspace. Below is how I use Notion to streamline client projects, manage creative workflows, and keep my business running smoothly?without relying on bloated tools or expensive software.

---

## Core Setup: The Foundation of My Notion Workspace

I built my Notion system around three pillars:

1. **Projects** ? All current and past client work

2. **Clients** ? Contact info, contracts, and communication history

3. **Resources** ? Brand guidelines, design assets, and inspiration

I started with a blank workspace and added databases for each pillar as separate pages. This keeps everything modular and easy to update.

### Key Databases I Use Daily

| Database Name | Purpose | Example Properties |

|---------------|---------|-------------------|

| **Projects** | Track active client work | Status, Client Name, Deadline, Priority, Design Files Link |

| **Clients** | Store contact details and contracts | Name, Email, Contract Date, Last Contact, Notes |

| **Brand Guidelines** | Centralize brand assets and rules | Client Name, Logo Files, Color Palette, Typography, Usage Rules |

| **Inspiration** | Save designs, color palettes, and trends | Image, Source Link, Category (e.g., "Logo Design", "Typography") |

| **Tasks** | Personal to-do list linked to projects | Task Name, Due Date, Project Association, Status |

## Client Management: Keeping Everything in One Place

Every new client starts in the **Clients** database. I create a dedicated page for them that links to their project, brand guidelines, and ongoing tasks.

### What I Include in Each Client Page

1. **Contact Information**

  • Email, phone number, and preferred communication method
  • Social media handles (if applicable)

2. **Contract & Payment Details**

  • Signed contract date and link to the file (I use Google Drive or Dropbox)
  • Payment terms, amount due, and payment status (e.g., "Paid", "Pending")

3. **Project Overview**

  • Brief summary of the project scope
  • Key stakeholders (e.g., "Marketing Director - Jane Doe")

4. **Communication Log**

  • A table of emails, messages, and meeting notes with dates
  • Example:

| Date | Type | Notes | Follow Up? |

|------|------|-------|------------|

| 2023-10-15 | Email | Client approved logo concepts | Yes - Send invoice |

5. **Next Steps**

  • A checklist of pending actions (e.g., "Send final invoice", "Schedule feedback session")

### How I Use Relations in Notion

I link the **Clients** database to the **Projects** database using a "relation" property. This means:

  • When I create a new project, I can select the client directly from a dropdown.
  • All projects for a client appear automatically under their page.
  • If I update a client?s contact info, it reflects everywhere they?re linked.

## Project Workflow: From Brief to Delivery

Each project lives in the **Projects** database. I break it into stages to mirror my design process:

### Project Stages & Notion Setup

| Stage | Notion Tools Used | Example Tasks |

|-------|-------------------|---------------|

| **Discovery** | Linked to client page, Inspiration database | Research competitors, gather brand references, draft project brief |

| **Concepting** | Kanban board (To Do, In Progress, Review) | Create mood boards, sketch logo ideas, design 3-5 variations |

| **Refinement** | Linked to brand guidelines, Tasks database | Adjust colors based on feedback, finalize typography, export assets |

| **Delivery** | Linked to client page, Payment tracking | Send final files, invoice client, archive project |

### Example: Designing a Logo

1. **Discovery Phase**

  • I create a new project entry in the **Projects** database.
  • I add a linked database block called "Discovery Notes" where I paste the client?s brief and my research (e.g., competitor logos, adjectives the client wants to evoke).

2. **Concepting Phase**

  • I create a **Kanban board** within the project page with columns for "To Do," "In Progress," and "Review."
  • I add each logo variation as a card with an image preview and a status label (e.g., "Concept 1 - Primary").
  • I link relevant inspiration from the **Inspiration** database to the project.

3. **Refinement Phase**

  • I update the Kanban board to "In Progress" for the selected concept.
  • I add a **Brand Guidelines** entry for the client with the final logo files, color codes, and typography rules.
  • I create a **Tasks** entry for "Export logo in multiple formats" with a due date.

4. **Delivery Phase**

  • I mark the project as "Complete" in the database.
  • I add a row to the **Clients** page under "Communication Log" noting the delivery.
  • I move the project to an "Archived Projects" sub-page for future reference.

## Brand Guidelines: Centralizing Design Assets

I use the **Brand Guidelines** database to store everything a client needs to maintain brand consistency. This includes:

1. **Logo Files**

  • SVG, PNG, and PDF versions
  • Minimum size requirements
  • Clear space guidelines (e.g., "Logo must be 2 inches clear from text")

2. **Color Palette**

  • Hex, RGB, and CMYK codes for each color
  • Examples of how colors should (and shouldn?t) be used

3. **Typography**

  • Primary and secondary fonts with weights
  • Example text samples (e.g., "Headline: Roboto Bold, Body: Open Sans Regular")

4. **Imagery & Iconography**

  • Approved photo styles (e.g., "Warm tones, candid shots")
  • Icon sets to use

5. **Do?s and Don?ts**

  • Examples of incorrect usage (e.g., "Never stretch the logo")

### How I Share Guidelines with Clients

I create a shareable Notion page for each client?s brand guidelines and:

  • Set permissions to "Comment" or "Read Only" to prevent accidental edits.
  • Include a "Download Assets" button that links to a Google Drive or Dropbox folder with all files.
  • Add a "Frequently Asked Questions" section for common questions (e.g., "How do I use the logo on a dark background?").

## Inspiration & Resources: Building a Design Vault

The **Inspiration** database is where I save anything that sparks creativity. I categorize entries to make them easy to find:

1. **Logo Design**

  • Examples of minimalist, vintage, or modern logo styles

2. **Typography**

  • Font pairings and layout examples

3. **Color Palettes**

  • Trends (e.g., "Earthy neutrals for 2024") or client-specific palettes

4. **Layouts**

  • Grid systems, business card designs, or website mockups

5. **Industry-Specific**

  • Examples from the client?s industry (e.g., "Tech startup branding")

### How I Use Inspiration

  • Before starting a new project, I filter the database by category to gather references.
  • I add a "Related Projects" relation to link inspiration directly to active projects.
  • I use the **Gallery view** to visually browse thumbnails, which is faster than scrolling through lists.

## Tasks & Time Management

I use the **Tasks** database to track personal deadlines and link them to projects. Each task includes:

| Property | Example |

|----------|---------|

| **Task Name** | "Create social media kit for Client X" |

| **Due Date** | 2023-11-01 |

| **Project** | Linked to the **Projects** database (e.g., "Client X - Brand Refresh") |

| **Status** | "In Progress", "On Hold", or "Complete" |

| **Priority** | "High", "Medium", or "Low" |

| **Time Estimate** | "2 hours" |

| **Notes** | "Need to confirm font weights with client" |

### Time Tracking with Notion

I add a **Number property** called "Time Spent (hours)" to log how long tasks take. This helps me:

  • Estimate future project timelines more accurately.
  • Identify bottlenecks in my workflow (e.g., "Logo revisions took 5 hours this time").

## Templates I Use to Save Time

I created reusable templates for common project types. Here?s how I set them up:

1. **New Client Onboarding**

  • Pre-filled databases for contact info, contract dates, and initial tasks.
  • Example tasks: "Send welcome email", "Schedule kickoff call".

2. **Logo Design Project**

  • Kanban board with stages: "Research" ? "Concepting" ? "Refinement" ? "Delivery".
  • Linked to a template **Brand Guidelines** entry.

3. **Social Media Kit**

  • Checklist for creating posts, banners, and profile images.
  • Linked to the client?s color palette and typography.

I duplicate these templates whenever I start a new project, then customize them as needed.

## Advanced Tips for Power Users

1. **Automate Repetitive Tasks**

  • Use **Notion?s API** with tools like Zapier or Make to connect Notion to your email or calendar.
  • Example: When a new email from a client arrives, create a task in Notion automatically.

2. **Collaborate with Clients**

  • Share project pages in "Comment" mode so clients can leave feedback directly on designs.
  • Use **Notion?s "Mention"** feature (@) to notify clients of updates.

3. **Backup Your Work**

  • Export your Notion workspace weekly to Google Drive or Dropbox as a backup.
  • Use **Notion?s "Export" function** to create a PDF or HTML backup of critical pages.

4. **Mobile-Friendly Workflow**

  • Download the Notion mobile app to update tasks, log hours, or check client details on the go.
  • Use **Notion?s "Offline Mode"** to access your workspace without internet.

## Why This System Works for Me

  • **Everything is connected**: No more switching between tools or losing files.
  • **Reduces mental load**: I spend less time organizing and more time designing.
  • **Scalable**: I can add new databases or templates as my business grows.
  • **Client-friendly**: Clients appreciate having a single source of truth for their brand.

Notion isn?t perfect?it lacks advanced time-tracking and invoicing?but it?s the closest thing to an all-in-one solution for freelance designers. The key is to start simple and refine as you go.
