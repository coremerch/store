pricing cheat sheet for motion designers ? 29 rules.

  1. Always calculate your base rate by dividing your desired annual income by 1,000 hours of billable work (Rule of 1,000).
  2. Charge 1.5x your base rate for rush projects with a 24-hour deadline.
  3. Use a project-based pricing model for small jobs under $2,000 to avoid scope creep.
  4. Implement a 50% deposit before starting any project to secure payment and commitment.
  5. Offer three pricing tiers (Basic, Pro, Premium) to guide clients toward your ideal package.
  6. Round up all time estimates to the nearest 15 minutes to avoid undercharging for small tasks.
  7. Add a 10-15% project management fee for clients who require frequent revisions or meetings.
  8. Use a day rate for large-scale projects (e.g., $800/day) to simplify billing for extended work.
  9. Negotiate a retainer model (e.g., $3,000/month for 20 hours) for ongoing client relationships.
  10. Charge a flat fee for revisions beyond the agreed-upon 2 rounds to discourage excessive changes.
  11. Factor in software costs (e.g., After Effects, Cinema 4D licenses) into your pricing for client transparency.
  12. Include a 5-10% buffer in your quote for unexpected revisions or delays caused by client feedback.
  13. Set a minimum project fee of $500 for one-off gigs to ensure profitability even for small jobs.
  14. Use a time-tracking tool (e.g., Toggl) to log every billable hour and justify your rates to clients.
  15. Offer a 5% discount for clients who pay within 7 days to incentivize prompt payments.
  16. Charge a 20% late fee for invoices paid after 30 days to enforce payment discipline.
  17. Break down your quote into line items (e.g., concept, animation, sound design) to justify your pricing.
  18. Always include a 10% contingency fee for projects with high client involvement or unclear scope.
  19. Use a non-refundable upfront fee (e.g., $200) to cover initial research or pre-production work.
  20. Charge extra for custom plugins, fonts, or assets you need to purchase for the project.
  21. Offer a 24-hour cancellation policy with a 50% non-refundable deposit to protect your time.
  22. Use a sliding scale for international clients based on their local cost of living (e.g., charge 1.2x for U.S. clients, 0.8x for clients in lower-cost countries).
  23. Include a clause in your contract for additional fees if the project scope expands beyond the original agreement.
  24. Charge a rush fee of 50% for projects due in less than 48 hours to compensate for overtime work.
  25. Use a hybrid pricing model (e.g., $50/hour + $1,000 project fee) for long-term collaborations with variable workloads.
  26. Always send a detailed invoice with a breakdown of hours, tasks, and expenses within 24 hours of project completion.
  27. Offer a 10% discount for clients who sign a 6-month retainer agreement upfront.
  28. Use a portfolio or case study fee (e.g., $300) for clients who request custom work for their portfolio without hiring you.
  29. Include a 15% industry-standard margin in your pricing to account for taxes, software upgrades, and business growth.
