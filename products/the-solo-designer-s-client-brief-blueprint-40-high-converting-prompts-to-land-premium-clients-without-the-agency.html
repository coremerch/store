The Solo Designer?s Client Brief Blueprint: 40 High-Converting Prompts to Land Premium Clients Without the Agency

client brief prompts for agency escapees going solo ? 40 prompts.

  1. List the top 3 types of clients you want to attract (e.g., eco-friendly startups, luxury brands, tech SaaS) and describe their pain points in one sentence each.
  2. Write the exact job title your ideal client uses on LinkedIn (e.g., 'Head of Brand at GreenTech Co') and explain why this role would hire you.
  3. Document the first question your ideal client asks when considering a new designer. Include the tone they use (e.g., 'Can you show me a portfolio with e-commerce experience?').
  4. Describe the format your client prefers for project updates (e.g., weekly Slack summaries, biweekly Notion boards, daily Trello cards).
  5. List three tools your client already uses for design collaboration (e.g., Figma, Adobe XD, Sketch) and explain how you?ll integrate into their workflow.
  6. Identify the biggest competitor your client fears and explain how your design style or process counters theirs in two sentences.
  7. Write the exact phrasing your client uses to describe their brand?s personality (e.g., 'playful yet sophisticated', 'minimalist with a bold edge').
  8. Describe the deliverable your client expects to receive on Day 1 of the project (e.g., mood board, wireframe, brand style guide draft).
  9. List the top 3 KPIs your client tracks for a successful design project (e.g., conversion rate, user engagement, brand recognition).
  10. Document the tone of voice your client uses in their emails (e.g., formal, casual, technical) and provide an example of how you?ll mirror it in your responses.
  11. Write the exact budget range your ideal client operates within (e.g., '$5K?$15K for branding', '$2K?$5K for a single landing page') and explain how you?ll position your pricing within it.
  12. List three objections your client might raise (e.g., 'We have an in-house designer', 'We?re not ready to commit yet') and write a one-sentence rebuttal for each.
  13. Describe the project timeline your client expects (e.g., '2-week turnaround for landing pages', '4 weeks for full rebrand') and explain how you?ll deliver ahead of schedule.
  14. Write the exact language your client uses to describe their target audience (e.g., 'millennial professionals aged 25?35', 'B2B software buyers').
  15. List three design trends your client either loves or hates (e.g., 'neon gradients are a no', 'we love subtle animations') and explain how you?ll incorporate or avoid them.
  16. Document the decision-making process in your client?s company (e.g., 'I approve all design work', 'Our CMO signs off') and name the person you need to impress most.
  17. Write the exact metrics your client uses to measure design success (e.g., 'CTR on our homepage', 'time spent on our product page').
  18. List three design assets your client already has (e.g., logo, brand colors, existing website) and explain how you?ll build upon or refine them.
  19. Describe the file formats your client requires for final deliverables (e.g., '.ai for logos', '.png for social media', '.sketch for web designs').
  20. Write the exact phrase your client uses to describe their project goals (e.g., 'We want to modernize our brand', 'We need a high-converting website').
  21. List three design tools your client is familiar with (e.g., Canva, Figma, Photoshop) and explain how you?ll use them collaboratively with them.
  22. Document the approval process for design work (e.g., 'I review, then send to the CEO for final sign-off') and name the person who gives the final green light.
  23. Write the exact language your client uses to describe their competitors (e.g., 'They have a dated website', 'Their brand feels impersonal').
  24. List three design elements your client wants to avoid (e.g., stock photos, Comic Sans font, cluttered layouts) and explain how you?ll address this in your work.
  25. Describe the onboarding process your client prefers (e.g., 'Send a welcome email with a questionnaire', 'Schedule a 30-minute kickoff call').
  26. Write the exact tone your client uses when giving feedback (e.g., 'Be bold with colors', 'Keep it clean and professional').
  27. List three design references your client has shared (e.g., Dribbble shots, competitor websites, Pinterest boards) and explain how you?ll incorporate their preferences.
  28. Document the communication channels your client prefers (e.g., email for formal updates, Slack for quick questions, Zoom for reviews).
  29. Write the exact phrasing your client uses to describe their brand colors (e.g., 'navy blue and gold', 'pantone 18-1750 TCX').
  30. List three design assets you?ll need from your client upfront (e.g., brand guidelines, existing marketing materials, product screenshots).
  31. Describe the revision process your client expects (e.g., 'We allow 2 rounds of revisions', 'Unlimited revisions for a flat fee').
  32. Write the exact language your client uses to describe their ideal customer journey (e.g., 'We want users to sign up in 3 clicks', 'Our homepage should tell our story instantly').
  33. List three design elements your client wants to highlight (e.g., our sustainability initiatives, our customer testimonials, our product features).
  34. Document the final delivery format your client expects (e.g., 'exported files in a Google Drive folder', 'a physical mockup for print').
  35. Write the exact phrasing your client uses to describe their brand voice (e.g., 'friendly and approachable', 'sophisticated and authoritative').
  36. List three design trends your client has used in the past (e.g., 'gradient overlays', '3D product renders') and explain whether you?ll continue or pivot from them.
  37. Describe the post-project follow-up your client expects (e.g., 'We?d like a 3-month check-in', 'We want a handover session').
  38. Write the exact language your client uses to describe their project budget constraints (e.g., 'We?re stretched thin this quarter', 'We have a $10K cap').
  39. List three design assets your client will need to provide mid-project (e.g., new product photos, updated copy, legal disclaimers).
  40. Document the stakeholder hierarchy for design approvals (e.g., 'Designer ? Marketing Manager ? CEO').
