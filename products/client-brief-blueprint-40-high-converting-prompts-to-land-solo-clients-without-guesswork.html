Client Brief Blueprint: 40 High-Converting Prompts to Land Solo Clients Without Guesswork

client brief prompts for agency escapees going solo ? 40 prompts.

  1. List the top 3 pain points your ideal client?s business faces (e.g., low conversion rates, outdated branding, poor user experience).
  2. Describe the exact type of project they?re looking for (e.g., website redesign, brand identity refresh, packaging design for a new product line).
  3. Provide 3 examples of brands they admire (not competitors) and explain why their design styles appeal to the client.
  4. Share 2-3 key performance indicators (KPIs) the client uses to measure success (e.g., increased sales, higher engagement, brand recognition).
  5. Specify the client?s budget range in clear terms (e.g., $5K?$10K for a full rebrand, $2K?$5K for a logo + business cards).
  6. Outline the client?s timeline for the project, including hard deadlines and milestones (e.g., phase 1: research by [date], phase 2: drafts by [date]).
  7. Identify the decision-makers involved in the project and their roles (e.g., CEO makes final calls, marketing director approves concepts).
  8. List the client?s competitors and describe one design element they?re currently doing better than the client.
  9. Provide 2-3 examples of past work the client loves (attach links or files if available) and explain what resonates with them.
  10. Clarify the client?s preferred communication style (e.g., weekly Slack updates, biweekly in-person meetings, or async email feedback).
  11. Describe the client?s brand voice and tone in 3 adjectives (e.g., professional, playful, minimalist) with examples of how it should translate visually.
  12. Specify any mandatory design elements the client insists on (e.g., must include the original logo mark, must use Pantone 186C for primary color).
  13. List 2-3 design trends the client dislikes (e.g., neon gradients, overly complex illustrations) and explain why they?re a turnoff.
  14. Provide the client?s target audience demographics (age, gender, location, income level) and psychographics (interests, values, lifestyle).
  15. Describe the client?s current brand assets (e.g., outdated logo, 300 DPI print files missing, no brand guidelines) and their limitations.
  16. Explain how the client expects the project to impact their business (e.g., attract younger audiences, modernize perception, increase trust).
  17. List any third-party tools or platforms the client uses that the design must integrate with (e.g., Shopify, Mailchimp, WordPress).
  18. Specify the format and resolution requirements for final deliverables (e.g., print-ready PDFs, web-optimized PNGs, vector files).
  19. Clarify if the client has a style guide or existing brand guidelines?and where to find them.
  20. Provide examples of past projects the client was unhappy with and explain what went wrong.
  21. Describe the client?s social media presence (platforms used, tone, visual consistency) and how the design should align with it.
  22. List any legal or compliance requirements the design must adhere to (e.g., FDA labeling, accessibility standards, copyright restrictions).
  23. Specify the client?s appetite for risk (e.g., prefer safe, tested designs vs. bold, experimental approaches) with examples.
  24. Provide the client?s preferred file naming conventions for deliverables (e.g., [ProjectName]_[ClientName]_[Version]_[Date]).
  25. List any industry-specific design standards the client must follow (e.g., medical device labeling, financial institution branding).
  26. Describe the client?s expectations for revisions (e.g., unlimited revisions, 3 rounds included in fee, additional rounds billed hourly).
  27. Explain how the client measures the success of a designer (e.g., reliability, creativity, adherence to deadlines).
  28. List the client?s top 3 competitors and describe one design element they?re missing that the client wants to incorporate.
  29. Provide the client?s preferred payment schedule (e.g., 50% upfront, 50% upon completion, or milestone-based payments).
  30. Describe any existing marketing materials the client wants the design to complement (e.g., trade show booth, social media ads).
  31. Clarify if the client has a preferred color palette or font family they want to use?and provide hex codes or file names.
  32. List any internal stakeholders the client wants to review the design before final approval (e.g., legal team, board of directors).
  33. Specify the client?s preferred file formats for deliverables (e.g., AI/PSD files, high-res JPGs, SVG files for web).
  34. Describe the client?s expectations for post-launch support (e.g., no support included, 30 days of tweaks, ongoing maintenance fee).
  35. Provide examples of design assets the client already has that should be preserved (e.g., mascot illustration, signature color).
  36. List any design elements the client wants to avoid (e.g., stock imagery, overly busy layouts, gradient overlays).
  37. Explain how the client wants to be credited for the project (e.g., in portfolio, case study, social media shoutout).
  38. Specify the client?s preferred tone for project updates (e.g., casual and friendly, formal and detailed, minimalist and to the point).
  39. Provide the client?s expectations for turnaround time on feedback (e.g., 48 hours for initial comments, 24 hours for revisions).
  40. List any industry awards or certifications the client wants the design to highlight (e.g., sustainable packaging, ADA-compliant website).
