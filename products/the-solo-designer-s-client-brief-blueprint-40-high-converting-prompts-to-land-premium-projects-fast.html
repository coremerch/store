The Solo Designer?s Client Brief Blueprint: 40 High-Converting Prompts to Land Premium Projects Fast

client brief prompts for agency escapees going solo ? 40 prompts.

  1. Ask the client to send 3 examples of brands they admire and explain why, then ask what specific design elements from those brands they want to emulate.
  2. Request a mood board with at least 5 images that represent the desired aesthetic, color palette, and overall vibe of the project.
  3. Have the client define their ideal customer avatar in one sentence, including demographics, pain points, and goals they want to achieve with the design.
  4. Ask for a list of 10 keywords that describe the brand?s personality and tone, then use those to guide the design direction.
  5. Request a competitor analysis with screenshots of 3 direct competitors? designs and ask the client to highlight what they like or dislike about each.
  6. Ask the client to provide a copy deck or key messaging they want included in the design, even if it?s just bullet points.
  7. Have the client outline the top 3 business objectives this project will support (e.g., increase conversions, build brand authority, launch a new product).
  8. Ask for their preferred color palette, including primary, secondary, and accent colors, with hex codes if possible.
  9. Request a list of 5 design references from any industry that inspire them, not just competitors, and ask what specifically draws them to each.
  10. Ask the client to describe their brand voice in 3 adjectives and provide examples of how that voice is currently communicated.
  11. Have the client define the primary and secondary calls-to-action for the project, including where they should be placed and what action they should drive.
  12. Ask for the target launch or delivery date and any hard deadlines that could impact the project timeline or revisions.
  13. Request a list of 3-5 must-have features or elements that the design absolutely cannot exclude, even if it means simplifying other areas.
  14. Ask the client to provide their brand?s mission statement or tagline to ensure the design aligns with their core messaging.
  15. Have the client identify their top 3 design pet peeves (e.g., cluttered layouts, neon color schemes, Comic Sans font) to avoid in this project.
  16. Ask for a breakdown of their ideal user journey through the design, including key touchpoints and interactions they want to highlight.
  17. Request a list of 5 adjectives that describe how they want their audience to feel when interacting with the design.
  18. Have the client define their budget range for this project upfront and ask if they have a preference for cost-saving measures or premium elements.
  19. Ask for their preferred typography style, including specific font names or categories (e.g., serif, sans-serif, handwritten) and any no-go fonts.
  20. Request a list of 3-5 past projects they?ve worked on that succeeded or failed, and ask what lessons should be applied to this design.
  21. Have the client outline the scope of work in their own words to ensure alignment on deliverables, timelines, and revisions.
  22. Ask for their preferred file formats and delivery requirements (e.g., Sketch files, SVG exports, print-ready PDFs).
  23. Request a list of 5 social media posts or marketing materials that represent their brand?s current style, even if informal.
  24. Have the client describe their ideal client interaction?do they want a sleek, minimalist experience or a bold, attention-grabbing one?
  25. Ask for any existing brand guidelines, style sheets, or previous design assets they want incorporated into the new project.
  26. Request a list of 3-5 industry trends they want to avoid or embrace in this design, with examples if possible.
  27. Have the client provide a rough sitemap or wireframe of their desired layout structure, even if it?s hand-drawn.
  28. Ask for their preferred image style (e.g., illustrative, photographic, abstract) and any stock photo subscriptions they have access to.
  29. Request a list of 5 websites or apps they find intuitive and easy to navigate, and ask what design elements make them user-friendly.
  30. Have the client define their ideal call-to-action placement?above the fold, mid-scroll, or at the end of a section?
  31. Ask for their preferred level of detail in the design: highly polished and finalized, or exploratory and iterative?
  32. Request a list of 3-5 technical requirements (e.g., mobile responsiveness, accessibility compliance, load speed optimization).
  33. Have the client outline any third-party integrations or tools that need to be compatible with the final design (e.g., CRM, payment gateways).
  34. Ask for their preferred tone for error messages, 404 pages, or loading states?humorous, professional, or minimalist?
  35. Request a list of 5 design tools they?re comfortable with or prefer the team use (e.g., Figma, Adobe XD, Illustrator).
  36. Have the client provide any customer feedback or testimonials they want highlighted in the design, along with context.
  37. Ask for their preferred approach to A/B testing: do they want to test multiple designs upfront or refine one based on analytics?
  38. Request a list of 3-5 industry-specific design conventions they want to follow or break, with reasoning.
  39. Have the client outline their preferred approval process?who needs to sign off, and in what order?
  40. Ask for any legal or compliance requirements (e.g., copyright notices, data protection disclaimers) that must be included in the design.
