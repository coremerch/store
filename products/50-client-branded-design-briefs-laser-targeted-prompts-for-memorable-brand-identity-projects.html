50 Client-Branded Design Briefs: Laser-Targeted Prompts for Memorable Brand Identity Projects

client brief prompts for brand identity designers ? 40 prompts.

  1. Ask the client to describe their brand?s personality in 3 adjectives and explain why each fits.
  2. Request a mood board of 10 images that visually represent the brand?s desired aesthetic.
  3. Have the client list 5 brands they admire and describe what they like about each.
  4. Ask for a written brand story in 100 words or less?what?s the brand?s origin, mission, and values?
  5. Require the client to provide 3 keywords that should instantly come to mind when people see their brand.
  6. Ask the client to share their top 3 competitors and what they do differently (or better).
  7. Request a list of 5 adjectives that describe how the client wants customers to feel when interacting with the brand.
  8. Ask for the brand?s color palette in hex codes?even if it?s aspirational, not yet finalized.
  9. Have the client describe their ideal customer in 3 sentences, including demographics and psychographics.
  10. Request a list of 5 fonts the client likes or dislikes?with examples or reasoning.
  11. Ask the client to provide 3 taglines or slogans they?ve considered or rejected.
  12. Have the client share any existing brand assets (logos, fonts, colors) they want to keep or modify.
  13. Request a breakdown of the brand?s tone of voice?formal, casual, humorous, authoritative, etc.
  14. Ask the client to describe their brand?s unique selling proposition (USP) in one sentence.
  15. Have the client list 3 design styles they hate and explain why.
  16. Ask for examples of brands with logos that resonate with them?what specifically do they like?
  17. Request a list of 5 design elements (shapes, textures, symbols) the client wants to incorporate or avoid.
  18. Ask the client to provide any industry-specific symbols or imagery they want to include in the identity.
  19. Have the client share their brand?s mission statement?if they have one?or draft one together.
  20. Request a list of 3 emotions the brand should evoke in its visual identity (e.g., trust, excitement, nostalgia).
  21. Ask the client to describe their brand?s visual hierarchy?what should stand out first, second, third?
  22. Have the client provide any cultural or regional considerations for the brand?s identity (e.g., symbolism, colors).
  23. Ask for a list of 5 brands whose identity they think is poorly designed and why.
  24. Request examples of packaging, business cards, or signage they?ve seen and admire.
  25. Ask the client to define their brand?s primary and secondary color palettes?even if not finalized.
  26. Have the client list 3 words that describe the brand?s visual style (e.g., minimalist, retro, futuristic).
  27. Ask for any existing brand guidelines or style sheets the client wants to reference or update.
  28. Request a list of 5 design trends they want to avoid (e.g., overused gradients, clich? stock imagery).
  29. Ask the client to describe their brand?s visual personality?playful, serious, elegant, rugged, etc.
  30. Have the client share any specific imagery they want to avoid (e.g., no animals, no medical symbols).
  31. Ask for examples of brands with identities that feel outdated?what would they change about them?
  32. Request a list of 3 textures or patterns the client associates with their brand?s identity.
  33. Ask the client to describe their brand?s ideal first impression?what should someone feel in the first 3 seconds?
  34. Have the client provide any religious, political, or social symbols they want to include or exclude.
  35. Ask for a list of 5 brands whose identities they think are timeless?what makes them effective?
  36. Request examples of typography they love or hate?with reasoning (e.g., readability, uniqueness).
  37. Ask the client to describe their brand?s visual consistency goals?how should it look across all touchpoints?
  38. Have the client share any industry-specific design constraints (e.g., FDA regulations, legal disclaimers).
  39. Ask for a list of 3 design elements the client associates with luxury or premium branding.
  40. Request examples of brands with identities that feel too generic?what would they do differently?
