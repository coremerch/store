pricing cheat sheet for design students ? 29 rules.

  1. Always research industry standards for your niche before quoting prices?never guess.
  2. Charge a 50% deposit upfront before starting any project to secure commitment.
  3. Use a flat fee for small projects (e.g., business cards, social media graphics) to simplify client understanding.
  4. Break larger projects into milestone payments (e.g., 30% upfront, 40% at halfway, 30% on delivery).
  5. Offer three pricing tiers (Basic, Standard, Premium) to guide clients and increase average order value.
  6. Never work for free?even for ?exposure??unless it?s a non-profit project with a signed contract.
  7. Add a 15-20% rush fee for clients who need designs completed in under 48 hours.
  8. Include a 10-15% late payment fee (clearly stated in your contract) to encourage timely payments.
  9. Bundle services (e.g., logo + business card + social media kit) to increase project value and client retention.
  10. Offer a ?maintenance package? (e.g., $50/month for updates) to generate recurring revenue from past clients.
  11. Charge by the hour for custom illustrations, detailed branding, or complex web designs where scope is unclear.
  12. Set a minimum project fee (e.g., $250) to avoid low-paying clients who waste your time.
  13. Always send a detailed contract before starting work, even for small projects, to protect both parties.
  14. Include a ?kill fee? (e.g., 30% of total project cost) if the client cancels mid-project to cover your time.
  15. Use a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana) to track deliverables and prevent scope creep without extra charges.
  16. Offer a ?first revision free, additional revisions cost $X? policy to manage client expectations and time efficiently.
  17. Upsell add-ons like social media templates, style guides, or print-ready files for an extra 20-30% fee.
  18. Charge a 10-15% fee for clients who want last-minute changes after the final draft is approved.
  19. Never negotiate your price down?offer alternatives (e.g., simplified scope, fewer revisions) instead of lowering fees.
  20. Use a pricing calculator (e.g., HoneyBook, Bonsai) to ensure your rates cover overhead, taxes, and profit.
  21. Offer a ?student discount? (e.g., 10-15%) only for portfolio pieces or non-commercial projects to build your resume.
  22. Include a ?revision limit? in your contract (e.g., 2 rounds included) to prevent endless tweaks.
  23. Charge extra for sourcing stock images ($10-$30 per image) or custom typography if clients request it.
  24. Use a ?value-based pricing? model for clients who see high ROI from your designs (e.g., e-commerce brands).
  25. Offer a ?quick turnaround? package (e.g., $300 for a 48-hour logo) to attract urgent clients willing to pay more.
  26. Always send an invoice immediately after project completion and follow up weekly until paid.
  27. Charge a 5-10% ?outsourcing fee? if you need to hire another designer or illustrator for part of the project.
  28. Set up a ?referral bonus? (e.g., $50 credit for every new client referred) to incentivize word-of-mouth growth.
  29. Include a ?late delivery fee? (e.g., $20/day) in your contract if you miss a deadline due to client delays in feedback.
