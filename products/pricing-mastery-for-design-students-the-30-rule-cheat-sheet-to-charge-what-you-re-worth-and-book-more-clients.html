Pricing Mastery for Design Students: The 30-Rule Cheat Sheet to Charge What You?re Worth and Book More Clients

pricing cheat sheet for design students ? 29 rules.

  1. Always ask for the project budget range first?never quote blindly.
  2. Research industry standards for your niche and location before sending a quote.
  3. Use a pricing calculator tool (like Hello Bonsai or Bonsai?s free templates) to avoid guesswork.
  4. Break down your quote into clear deliverables (e.g., 3 logo concepts, 5 social media templates).
  5. Charge a flat fee for small projects (business cards, flyers) to save time on negotiations.
  6. Offer 3 pricing tiers (Basic, Pro, Premium) with clear differences in scope and extras.
  7. Add a 15-20% rush fee for projects with a tight deadline under 48 hours.
  8. Include a 50% upfront payment for projects over $500 to secure commitment.
  9. Never work for free?offer a discounted portfolio piece instead of full pro bono work.
  10. Use a contract template (like HelloSign or DocuSign) to outline payment terms and revisions.
  11. Factor in your time to learn new tools or software into your hourly rate.
  12. Round your quotes up to the nearest $50 for simplicity and perceived value.
  13. Offer a 10% discount for clients who pay within 7 days to encourage prompt payments.
  14. Explain your pricing honestly: ?My rate reflects my 2 years of design training and 50+ hours of work.?
  15. Create a ?starter package? for clients who balk at full pricing?e.g., a single social post for $150.
  16. Use a project scope checklist to avoid ?scope creep? and unpaid extra work.
  17. Charge per revision (e.g., 2 revisions included, $50 each after) to manage client expectations.
  18. Factor in overhead costs (software subscriptions, internet, workspace) into your rates.
  19. Offer a ?portfolio refresh? service at a lower rate to attract new clients and gain testimonials.
  20. Use the ?$1 per hour of work? rule for beginners?e.g., a 10-hour project = $1000 minimum.
  21. Exclude taxes from quotes initially, then add them at checkout to avoid sticker shock.
  22. Set a minimum project fee ($200 for students) to avoid low-balling yourself on small gigs.
  23. Use past client budgets as a reference?ask peers for anonymous rate ranges in design groups.
  24. Offer a ?rush + revision? add-on for clients who need last-minute changes ($75 extra).
  25. Charge a 10% late fee after the payment due date to enforce timely payments.
  26. Create a ?membership? option for repeat clients (e.g., 5 social posts/month for $800).
  27. Use a time-tracking tool (like Toggl) to justify hourly rates in client conversations.
  28. Always follow up with a polite email summarizing the quote, timeline, and next steps.
  29. End quotes with a deadline (e.g., ?Offer valid for 7 days?) to prompt quick decisions.
