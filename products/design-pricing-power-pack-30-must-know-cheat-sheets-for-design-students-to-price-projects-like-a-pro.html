Design Pricing Power Pack: 30 Must-Know Cheat Sheets for Design Students to Price Projects Like a Pro

pricing cheat sheet for design students ? 29 rules.

  1. List every deliverable in the scope of work to avoid scope creep; include revisions and final files.
  2. Use a flat fee for small projects like logos, business cards, or social media graphics to keep it simple.
  3. Charge per hour for ongoing or open-ended tasks like branding development or illustration series.
  4. Research average rates for your niche and location using platforms like AIGA or local design groups.
  5. Break down complex projects into phases (e.g., research, sketches, final designs) and price each separately.
  6. Add a 10-20% buffer to your estimated hours to account for unexpected revisions or delays.
  7. For student discounts, clearly state the conditions (e.g., ?limited to 3 revisions? or ?non-commercial use only?).
  8. Offer package deals for multiple services (e.g., logo + business card + social media templates) at a 15% discount.
  9. Charge a 50% deposit before starting any project to secure your time and resources.
  10. Include a 5-10% late fee for payments made past the agreed due date to encourage timely payments.
  11. Use a contract template for every project to outline scope, deadlines, payment terms, and ownership rights.
  12. For print projects, calculate costs based on print quantity, paper type, and finishing options (e.g., foil stamping).
  13. Price digital products (e.g., templates, icons) based on market demand, uniqueness, and licensing terms.
  14. Offer a rush fee (e.g., 20-30% extra) for projects with tight deadlines (less than 48 hours).
  15. Create a ?rush fee? tier system: 24-hour rush (30%), 12-hour rush (50%), same-day rush (100%).
  16. Charge extra for custom illustrations or complex graphic elements that require additional time.
  17. Use a sliding scale for charity work: offer 50% off for nonprofits but cap revisions at 2.
  18. For subscription-based work (e.g., monthly social media graphics), charge a flat monthly retainer with a 3-month minimum.
  19. Price stock photo licenses separately if you?re including them in the design (e.g., $10 per image).
  20. Offer a ?first-time client? discount (e.g., 10%) to build your portfolio and client base.
  21. For web design, charge a one-time setup fee plus a monthly maintenance fee for updates and hosting.
  22. Use a project management tool like Trello or Asana to track time and milestones for accurate billing.
  23. Charge for client meetings that exceed 1 hour separately (e.g., $50 per hour).
  24. Price color corrections or additional file formats (e.g., SVG, EPS) as add-ons (e.g., $20 per format).
  25. For branding projects, include a ?brand guidelines? document as a separate line item ($50-$100).
  26. Offer a ?portfolio package? for design students: 5 custom pieces (e.g., resume, business card, social media kit) for $300.
  27. Charge a ?kill fee? (e.g., 30% of total project cost) if the client cancels after significant work has begun.
  28. Include a ?revision round? limit in your contract (e.g., 3 rounds included, $50 per additional round).
  29. Use a tiered pricing model for logo design: basic ($150), premium ($300), deluxe ($500) with increasing features.
