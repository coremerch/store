Freelance Design Pricing Cheat Sheet: From Student to Pro in 30 Simple Rules

pricing cheat sheet for design students ? 29 rules.

  1. Price new clients with a 50% deposit before starting any work to ensure commitment and cash flow.
  2. Charge hourly for revisions beyond the agreed scope to protect your time and profitability.
  3. Offer three package tiers (Basic, Standard, Premium) with clear deliverables and fixed prices to simplify client decisions.
  4. Use the 'Rule of Thirds' pricing model: 1/3 for expenses, 1/3 for profit, and 1/3 for your rate to ensure sustainability.
  5. Always ask for a brief before quoting?never give a ballpark estimate without understanding the project fully.
  6. Research local market rates for similar services and adjust your pricing to stay competitive while valuing your skills.
  7. Set a minimum project fee of $150 for small tasks to avoid undervaluing your work and wasting time on unprofitable gigs.
  8. Offer 10% off for clients who pay upfront in full to incentivize quick payments and improve your cash flow.
  9. Use a contract with a kill fee clause (typically 30-50% of the total project cost) if the client cancels mid-project.
  10. Track every hour spent on a project and invoice accordingly?clients respect transparency and are less likely to dispute fair billing.
  11. Charge extra for expedited delivery (e.g., 20% premium for projects completed in half the standard time).
  12. Bundle services (e.g., logo design + business card + social media kit) to increase average order value by 25-40%.
  13. Set a late fee of 1.5% per week on overdue invoices to encourage timely payments and protect your income.
  14. Offer a 'rush fee' of $50-$200 for last-minute projects to compensate for the disruption to your schedule.
  15. Use the 'Cost-Based Pricing' formula: (Hourly Rate ? Estimated Hours) + 15% Buffer for unexpected delays or revisions.
  16. Create a 'Not-To-Do' list of unpaid or low-paying clients to avoid wasting time on projects that don?t align with your worth.
  17. Charge 20% more for clients outside your timezone due to coordination challenges and potential overtime work.
  18. Offer a 'maintenance package' (e.g., $50/month for 1 hour of design tweaks) to generate recurring revenue from past clients.
  19. Use a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana) to track deliverables and bill accurately for work completed.
  20. Set a 'design audit' fee of $100-$300 to review and provide actionable feedback for clients? existing materials before taking on new work.
  21. Charge a $25-$50 'consultation fee' for initial meetings to filter serious clients and cover your time investment.
  22. Offer a 'scaled pricing' model for startups: lower initial rates in exchange for equity or future revenue share.
  23. Use the 'Value-Based Pricing' approach: charge based on the client?s perceived ROI of your design work, not just your time.
  24. Implement a 'first-time client discount' of 10-15% to attract new customers, but never offer this discount again for repeat work.
  25. Set a 'minimum engagement' of 10 hours for new clients to avoid one-off, low-value projects that drain your resources.
  26. Charge 15% more for clients who require unlimited revisions to compensate for the extra time and frustration.
  27. Offer a 'portfolio development fee' of $200-$500 for students to create high-quality work for their portfolio in exchange for a testimonial and case study.
  28. Use a 'design sprint' pricing model: charge $500-$1,500 for a 3-day intensive design session to produce quick, high-value outcomes for clients.
