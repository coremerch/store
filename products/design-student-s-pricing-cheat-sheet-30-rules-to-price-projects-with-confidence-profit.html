Design Student's Pricing Cheat Sheet: 30 Rules to Price Projects with Confidence & Profit

pricing cheat sheet for design students ? 29 rules.

  1. Charge $20?$50/hour for beginner projects like logos or social media graphics, adjusting based on complexity and client budget.
  2. Set a flat fee of $150?$300 for a single logo design with 3 revisions included to simplify pricing for small clients.
  3. Offer a 'Student Discount' of 10?20% for portfolio work but never below your minimum viable rate to maintain value perception.
  4. Use a 50% deposit for custom design projects to secure payment before starting work, protecting against last-minute cancellations.
  5. Price social media templates at $50?$150 per set, factoring in time spent designing and the client?s potential reuse.
  6. Create a 'Quick Turnaround' fee add-on of 20?30% for rush jobs delivered in under 48 hours to compensate for overtime effort.
  7. Bundle 3?5 social media posts for $200?$400 to encourage clients to invest in a full campaign rather than single posts.
  8. Charge $100?$250 for a basic website landing page using platforms like Webflow or Figma, based on design complexity and interactions.
  9. Offer a 'Maintenance Package' at $50?$100/month for updates, edits, or content refreshes to generate recurring revenue.
  10. Price a full brand identity package at $500?$1,200, breaking it into phases (logo, color palette, typography, guidelines) to justify the cost.
  11. Use a 'Project Scope Document' to outline deliverables, revisions, and fees upfront to avoid scope creep and disputes.
  12. Charge $75?$150 for a single social media ad design, with add-ons for A/B testing or animated elements adding $50?$100.
  13. Set a 'Kill Fee' of 20?30% of the project cost if the client cancels after you?ve started working to cover sunk costs.
  14. Price infographics at $150?$300, factoring in research, data visualization, and the client?s need for clarity and engagement.
  15. Offer a 'First-Time Client' discount of 15% to attract new customers but include a clause that rates increase for repeat work.
  16. Charge $200?$500 for a full website redesign, segmented by pages (homepage, about, contact) to make pricing transparent.
  17. Use a 'Retainer Model' of $300?$800/month for ongoing design needs, ensuring steady income and client loyalty.
  18. Price UI/UX wireframes at $100?$250 per screen, with higher fees for interactive prototypes or user research add-ons.
  19. Set a 'Minimum Project Fee' of $50 to avoid wasting time on tiny, low-paying gigs; reject jobs under this threshold.
  20. Charge $100?$300 for a print-ready flyer or brochure, including high-resolution files and bleed settings for professional output.
  21. Offer a 'Referral Bonus' of $20?$50 for clients who bring in new business to incentivize word-of-mouth marketing.
  22. Price packaging designs at $300?$800, depending on complexity, mockups, and the client?s need for physical product visualization.
  23. Use a 'Late Payment Fee' of 1.5% per week on overdue invoices to encourage timely payments without being overly aggressive.
  24. Charge $50?$150 for a social media cover photo or profile picture design, with add-ons for animations or custom illustrations.
  25. Set a 'Revision Limit' (e.g., 3 revisions) per project to cap your time investment and prevent endless tweaks.
  26. Price a 30-minute 'Design Consultation' at $40?$80 to offer advice without committing to a full project.
  27. Offer a 'Seasonal Discount' of 10?15% during slow months (e.g., January or August) to attract clients when demand is low.
  28. Charge $200?$500 for a custom illustration pack (e.g., 5?10 icons or characters) with unlimited commercial use rights.
  29. Use a 'Project Management Fee' of 5?10% for large projects to cover coordination, communications, and client management overhead.
