Freelance Designer's Pricing Cheat Sheet: From Student to Pro in 30 Steps

pricing cheat sheet for design students ? 29 rules.

  1. Always research local market rates before quoting a project to stay competitive but profitable.
  2. Use a rate calculator tool to determine your minimum hourly rate based on your living expenses and desired profit margin.
  3. Charge a 50% deposit before starting any project to ensure commitment and reduce financial risk.
  4. Offer three pricing tiers (Basic, Standard, Premium) to give clients options and increase perceived value.
  5. Round your quotes to the nearest $50 or $100 to make them feel more professional and easier to approve.
  6. Factor in revision rounds?include 1-2 free rounds in your base price, then charge per additional revision.
  7. Use a project brief template to clarify scope, deliverables, and expectations before quoting a price.
  8. Charge extra for rush jobs?add 20-50% to your standard rate for tight deadlines.
  9. Create an hourly rate for consulting calls or brainstorming sessions separate from design work.
  10. Offer a rush fee waiver if the client commits to a longer timeline, incentivizing better planning.
  11. Break down your pricing into line items (e.g., research, sketching, final design) to justify costs transparently.
  12. Use a flat fee for small, repetitive tasks like social media posts or business card designs to save time on quoting.
  13. Charge a cancellation fee (e.g., 50% of the deposit) if the client backs out after the agreement is signed.
  14. Include a clause in your contract that allows you to increase prices by 5-10% annually to account for inflation.
  15. Offer a discount for long-term clients who commit to multiple projects upfront (e.g., 10% off for 3+ projects).
  16. Use a value-based pricing model for high-impact designs (e.g., branding for a startup) instead of hourly or flat rates.
  17. Set a minimum project fee (e.g., $200) to avoid undervaluing your time on small tasks.
  18. Charge a setup fee for new clients to cover administrative costs like contracts and invoicing.
  19. Use a retainer model for ongoing work (e.g., $X/month for unlimited revisions or 10 hours of design work).
  20. Offer a portfolio package discount?e.g., 20% off if the client hires you for both a logo and a website design.
  21. Charge extra for stock photos, fonts, or third-party assets used in the design (e.g., +$50 for premium fonts).
  22. Include a clause in your contract that allows you to increase rates by 15% if the project scope expands beyond the original agreement.
  23. Use a sliding scale for non-profits or students?e.g., 10% off for verified non-profit organizations.
  24. Charge a design audit fee (e.g., $150) to review and suggest improvements for a client?s existing branding before a redesign.
  25. Offer a loyalty program?e.g., after 5 completed projects, give the client a free social media graphic or minor update.
  26. Use a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana) to track time and justify hourly rates to clients.
  27. Charge a rush fee for last-minute changes after the project deadline has passed (e.g., +30% of the original quote).
  28. Include a clause in your contract that allows you to keep 50% of the deposit if the client ghosts you mid-project.
  29. Offer a referral bonus?e.g., $50 credit or 10% off their next project for every client they refer who books a design job.
