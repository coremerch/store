Design Pricing Cheat Sheet

pricing cheat sheet for freelance designer pricing ? 29 rules.

  1. Charge a flat project fee only if the scope is locked and documented in a signed contract.
  2. Offer 3 pricing tiers (Basic, Pro, Enterprise) with clear deliverables for each to upsell clients.
  3. Always calculate your hourly rate by dividing your target annual income by 1,000 productive hours (not 2,000 total hours).
  4. Add a 15-20% buffer to your estimated hours to account for revisions and scope creep.
  5. Use a 50% deposit before starting any work to filter serious clients and reduce financial risk.
  6. Implement a late payment fee of 1.5% per month (18% annually) to incentivize on-time payments.
  7. Bill weekly or bi-weekly instead of monthly to maintain cash flow and reduce payment delays.
  8. Offer a 5-10% discount for clients who pay the full amount upfront to improve your liquidity.
  9. Never work for free, but offer a free 30-minute consultation to qualify leads and set expectations.
  10. Use value-based pricing: charge based on the client?s perceived ROI, not just your time or effort.
  11. For retainers, price at 60-70% of your average project fee to lock in recurring revenue with less effort.
  12. Always include a scope of work document in every contract to prevent misunderstandings and unpaid extra work.
  13. Charge a rush fee of 25-50% for projects due in less than 48 hours to compensate for lost flexibility.
  14. For logo design, charge a flat fee per revision (e.g., $50 per revision after 2 included), not a percentage of the project.
  15. Use a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana) to track time and bill accurately; clients respect transparency.
  16. Set a minimum project fee (e.g., $500) to avoid wasting time on low-budget clients who won?t value your work.
  17. Offer a payment plan for clients who can?t afford the full amount upfront, but include a processing fee (e.g., 3%).
  18. Always send a detailed invoice with a breakdown of hours, tasks, and deliverables to justify your pricing.
  19. For web design, charge extra for custom animations, integrations, or third-party plugins that increase your workload.
  20. Use a contract template (e.g., from HelloSign or Bonsai) to ensure legal protection and professionalism in every deal.
  21. For social media graphics, price per post or per month, not per hour, since output is more predictable than creative time.
  22. Charge a 10-15% premium for clients who want exclusive rights to your designs (e.g., logo ownership transfer).
  23. Track your actual hours vs. estimated hours for each project and adjust pricing for similar projects in the future.
  24. For print design, include setup fees for printers or third parties to avoid absorbing unexpected costs later.
  25. Use a 20% down payment for custom illustration or branding projects to secure the client?s commitment.
  26. Offer a free audit of a competitor?s website or branding to showcase your expertise and justify your pricing.
  27. For e-commerce design, charge a percentage (e.g., 1-2%) of projected sales if the design directly impacts revenue.
  28. Always ask clients for testimonials or referrals in exchange for a small discount on their next project.
  29. End every project with a post-mortem: review what worked, what didn?t, and adjust pricing strategies accordingly.
