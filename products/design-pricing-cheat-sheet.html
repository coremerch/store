Design Pricing Cheat Sheet

pricing cheat sheet for freelance designer pricing ? 29 rules.

  1. Always calculate your hourly rate by dividing your desired annual income by 1,000 billable hours (not 2,080 total hours), ensuring realistic profit margins.
  2. Use a flat-rate pricing model for small projects under 10 hours to simplify invoicing and reduce client negotiation friction.
  3. Charge a 50% deposit before starting any project over $500 to secure commitment and cover initial costs immediately.
  4. Apply a 15% rush fee for projects with deadlines under 48 hours to compensate for overtime and disrupted workflow.
  5. Break large projects into milestone-based payments (e.g., 30% upfront, 40% at midpoint, 30% on delivery) to maintain cash flow consistency.
  6. Add a 10% client management fee for projects requiring extensive revisions or off-hours communication to offset time spent outside core work.
  7. Use a design brief questionnaire to scope projects accurately and include a clause in your contract that revisions beyond 3 are charged at $75/hour.
  8. Never undercut your rates for speculative work?always charge a design fee upfront or offer a paid discovery session instead.
  9. Implement a 20% project management fee for clients who micromanage or require frequent check-ins, reflecting the added administrative burden.
  10. Offer tiered pricing packages (Basic, Pro, Premium) with clearly defined deliverables, deadlines, and add-on costs to guide client decisions.
  11. Charge a 10% late fee on overdue invoices after 15 days to incentivize timely payments and protect your cash flow.
  12. Use a project estimation tool like FreshBooks or Harvest to track time spent vs. billed hours in real-time and adjust rates accordingly.
  13. Apply a 5% international transaction fee for clients paying via PayPal or credit cards that incur high processing costs (2.9% + $0.30).
  14. Set a minimum project fee of $300 for logo designs to ensure profitability, even for quick turnaround clients.
  15. Offer a 5% discount for clients who pay 100% upfront to encourage early payments and reduce financial risk on your end.
  16. Use a contract clause that allows you to increase rates by 5-10% annually to account for inflation and growing expertise.
  17. Charge a 15% design license fee for commercial-use templates or assets that clients can reuse beyond the initial project scope.
  18. Implement a 24-hour response window for all client inquiries to manage expectations and avoid scope creep from excessive communication.
  19. Use a retainer model for ongoing clients (e.g., $1,500/month for 10 hours of design work) to secure steady income and reduce sales effort.
  20. Apply a 10% emergency revision fee for changes requested after final delivery, as these typically require reworking completed work.
  21. Always include a clause in your contract that outlines kill fees (e.g., 50% of the project cost) if the client cancels after significant work has begun.
  22. Charge a 5% project complexity fee for clients who require custom illustrations, animations, or advanced technical integrations beyond standard design.
  23. Use a project scope document that explicitly lists excluded services (e.g., social media management, copywriting) to avoid unpaid extra work.
  24. Offer a 10% referral bonus to clients who bring in new business to incentivize word-of-mouth marketing and reduce acquisition costs.
  25. Apply a 15% premium for projects that require urgent turnaround within 24 hours, reflecting the opportunity cost of other work.
  26. Use a value-based pricing model for high-impact projects (e.g., rebranding for a Fortune 500 company) by charging a percentage of the client?s projected ROI (e.g., 0.5-2%).
  27. Charge a 10% file organization fee for delivering project assets in a structured, client-friendly format (e.g., organized layers, named folders).
  28. Include a 5% technology fee for using premium software (e.g., Adobe Creative Cloud, Figma) that you pass through to the client at cost.
  29. Always send a final invoice with a clear breakdown of hours worked, expenses, and deliverables within 48 hours of project completion to expedite payment.
