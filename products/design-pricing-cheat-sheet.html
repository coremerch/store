Design Pricing Cheat Sheet

pricing cheat sheet for freelance designer pricing ? 30 rules.

  1. Always research local and industry-standard hourly rates for your niche using platforms like Glassdoor, Payscale, or AIGA?s salary calculator before setting your own rates.
  2. Charge per project, not hourly, for clients who value results over time spent?use a fixed-price contract with clear deliverables and scope.
  3. Offer three pricing tiers (Basic, Pro, Premium) to guide clients toward higher-value packages without negotiation fatigue.
  4. Calculate your desired annual income, subtract business expenses, divide by 1,500 billable hours (industry standard), then round up to set your minimum hourly rate.
  5. For logo design, charge at least $500?$2,000 depending on complexity, client budget, and your experience level?never accept less than $300 for a basic logo.
  6. Use the ?value-based pricing? model: estimate the client?s potential ROI from your design and charge 10?20% of that projected gain.
  7. For web design projects, charge $2,000?$10,000+ based on page count, functionality, and integrations?never accept a project under $1,500 unless it?s a personal portfolio piece.
  8. Include a 50% deposit before starting any project to filter serious clients and protect against non-payment.
  9. Add a 15?20% ?rush fee? for projects with tight deadlines (under 48 hours), clearly stated in your contract.
  10. Offer monthly retainers for ongoing design needs (e.g., social media graphics, email templates) at $500?$2,000/month depending on workload and exclusivity.
  11. Never work on spec?always charge for revisions beyond the agreed-upon scope, with a flat fee per additional revision (e.g., $100 per extra round).
  12. For branding packages, bundle services (logo, color palette, typography guide, business card design) and price them at 20?30% less than individual services combined.
  13. Use the ?per page? pricing model for print design (e.g., $150?$300 per page for brochures or catalogs) to simplify quoting.
  14. Charge extra for stock photos, fonts, or third-party assets used in client projects?itemize these costs and markup by 20?30%.
  15. For UI/UX design, base your rate on the project?s complexity: $1,500?$5,000 for wireframes, $3,000?$10,000 for full prototypes with user testing.
  16. Always include a 24?48 hour ?cooling-off? period in your contract where clients can cancel without penalty?this builds trust and reduces last-minute cancellations.
  17. For social media design, charge $200?$800 per post or $1,500?$5,000/month for a full content calendar and graphics.
  18. Use the ?cost-plus? pricing method for print work: calculate material costs + 30?50% markup for labor and profit.
  19. For packaging design, charge $1,000?$5,000+ depending on the product?s retail value and mockup complexity?never undervalue this service.
  20. Offer a ?maintenance package? for post-launch support (e.g., $100?$300/month for minor updates or troubleshooting).
  21. For e-commerce design (Shopify, WooCommerce), charge $2,000?$8,000 for a custom theme, plus $100?$300/hour for custom development.
  22. Always get a signed contract before starting work?use templates from HelloSign or DocuSign to streamline the process.
  23. For motion graphics or video editing, charge $500?$3,000 per minute of final output, depending on complexity and your toolset (After Effects, Premiere Pro, etc.).
  24. Use the ?day rate? model for on-site or intensive projects: $500?$1,200/day for local clients, $800?$2,000/day for remote or high-stakes projects.
  25. For infographic design, charge $300?$1,500 based on data complexity, research requirements, and custom illustrations needed.
  26. Never discount your rates for friends or family?offer a referral bonus instead (e.g., 10% off their next project for every new client they send).
  27. For annual reports or high-stakes corporate design, charge $5,000?$20,000+ based on the client?s revenue size and design complexity.
  28. Use the ?percentage of total budget? model for clients who struggle with fixed pricing: charge 15?25% of their allocated design budget.
  29. For email template design, charge $200?$800 per template set (HTML + mobile-responsive), with add-ons for A/B testing or automation setup.
  30. Always include a ?kill fee? in your contract (e.g., 20?30% of total project cost) if the client cancels mid-project to cover your lost time and resources.
