Motion Designer?s Pricing Playbook: 30 Proven Rules to Price Projects for Maximum Profit & Client Buy-In

pricing cheat sheet for motion designers ? 30 rules.

  1. Always charge per second for motion graphics projects (e.g., $200?$500 per second for high-end work, $50?$150 for mid-tier).
  2. For explainer videos, use a flat rate based on length: $1,000?$3,000 for 60 seconds, $2,500?$6,000 for 90 seconds.
  3. Break down social media motion content (Reels, TikTok, Stories) into micro-projects priced at $150?$400 per 15-second clip.
  4. Charge a 50% deposit before starting any motion project to secure commitment and cover initial costs.
  5. For logo animations, set a fixed price: $300?$800 for simple, $1,200?$3,000 for custom or branded sequences.
  6. Offer package deals for social media content?e.g., 5 Reels for $1,500 (30% discount vs. individual pricing).
  7. Use a tiered pricing structure for corporate videos: Basic ($2,000?$5,000), Pro ($5,000?$12,000), Premium ($12,000+).
  8. For UI/UX motion micro-interactions, charge $500?$2,000 per interaction based on complexity and integration time.
  9. Always include a 10?15% buffer in quotes for revisions beyond the agreed-upon scope to avoid scope creep.
  10. For animated ads (Facebook, Instagram, YouTube), price per second: $100?$300 for dynamic ads, $300?$800 for premium campaigns.
  11. Offer retainers for ongoing motion work?e.g., $2,000?$5,000/month for 4?8 social media clips or updates.
  12. Charge extra for custom sound design ($200?$500) or licensed music ($50?$200 per track) if not included in the base price.
  13. For product demo videos, base pricing on complexity: $1,500?$4,000 for static product demos, $4,000?$10,000 for interactive animations.
  14. Set a minimum project fee of $500 for any motion work to ensure profitability, even for small clients.
  15. For animated infographics, price per key visual: $200?$600 for simple, $600?$1,500 for data-heavy or complex.
  16. Always provide a detailed breakdown of hours and tasks in your contract to justify your pricing and prevent disputes.
  17. For event openers or closers, charge a flat rate: $800?$2,000 for 30?60 seconds, $2,000?$5,000 for high-end cinematic versions.
  18. Offer a rush fee (20?50% extra) for projects due within 48 hours to compensate for overtime and priority scheduling.
  19. For animated email signatures, charge $300?$800 depending on style (GIF, HTML5, or custom branding).
  20. Use a sliding scale for recurring clients?e.g., 10% discount after 3 projects, 15% after 5 projects.
  21. For motion graphics in presentations (PowerPoint, Keynote), price per slide: $150?$400 for simple, $400?$1,000 for custom animations.
  22. Charge a setup fee ($100?$300) for new clients to cover onboarding, contracts, and initial project setup.
  23. For animated GIFs (e.g., for blogs or ads), price per GIF: $100?$300 for standard, $300?$800 for high-resolution or multi-frame.
  24. Include a 30-day post-delivery support window in your pricing to handle minor fixes without additional fees.
  25. For motion branding packages (logo animations + brand guidelines), charge $2,500?$8,000 based on brand complexity.
  26. Price 3D motion graphics projects at $500?$1,500 per second for basic renderings, $1,500?$4,000 for photorealistic work.
  27. For animated countdowns or timers, charge $200?$600 for standard, $600?$1,500 for dynamic or interactive versions.
  28. Use a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Notion) to track hours and justify pricing if clients question your rates.
  29. For motion graphics in e-learning modules, price per minute: $300?$800 for simple interactions, $800?$2,000 for complex animations.
  30. Always send a formal proposal with clear deliverables, timelines, and pricing before starting any motion project to avoid misunderstandings.
