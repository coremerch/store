Motion Designer?s Pricing Playbook: 30 Rules to Price Projects with Confidence and Profit

pricing cheat sheet for motion designers ? 30 rules.

  1. List every deliverable in the project scope to avoid scope creep?client signs off on the full list before work begins.
  2. Break down projects into hourly, daily, or milestone-based pricing to align with client expectations and cash flow needs.
  3. Use a flat fee for small, clearly defined projects (e.g., a 30-second social media ad) to simplify invoicing and client understanding.
  4. Charge a 20?30% deposit before starting any project to filter serious clients and cover initial costs.
  5. Add a rush fee (e.g., 25?50% extra) for projects with tight deadlines to compensate for overtime and expedited resources.
  6. Implement a late fee (e.g., 1.5% per week) on overdue invoices to encourage timely payments without damaging client relationships.
  7. Offer tiered pricing for revisions (e.g., 2 rounds included, $X per additional round) to set clear boundaries and manage expectations.
  8. Calculate your effective hourly rate by dividing total project revenue by actual hours worked, then adjust pricing accordingly for future projects.
  9. Use a project timeline tool (e.g., Trello, Asana) to log hours and track progress, ensuring accurate billing and client transparency.
  10. Research industry benchmarks for motion design rates in your niche (e.g., explainer videos, UI animations) to price competitively yet profitably.
  11. Charge a 10?15% project management fee if you?re coordinating with other vendors (e.g., voice actors, composers) to cover coordination overhead.
  12. Set a minimum project fee (e.g., $500) to avoid undercharging for small or time-consuming tasks like logo animations.
  13. Offer retainer packages (e.g., $X/month for 10 hours of motion work) to secure recurring income and stable client relationships.
  14. Include a kill fee (e.g., 30?50% of project cost) if the client cancels mid-project to compensate for lost time and resources.
  15. Use a contract template that outlines payment terms, deliverables, revisions, and ownership rights to protect both parties legally.
  16. Price motion graphics for social media (e.g., Instagram Reels, TikTok) differently from broadcast or corporate videos due to platform-specific expectations.
  17. Factor in software costs (e.g., Adobe After Effects, Cinema 4D licenses) into your pricing to ensure you?re not subsidizing client expenses.
  18. Charge extra for custom assets (e.g., unique 3D models, custom typography) that require additional design time beyond templates.
  19. Offer a discount (e.g., 5?10%) for clients who pay upfront in full to improve your cash flow and reduce payment chasing.
  20. Set a 48-hour response time for client communications to manage expectations and avoid last-minute changes that derail schedules.
  21. Use a project scoping questionnaire to gather all necessary details upfront, reducing the risk of miscommunication and revisions later.
  22. Price motion design for live events (e.g., trade shows, conferences) higher due to the need for on-site adjustments and real-time collaboration.
  23. Charge a setup fee (e.g., $200) for new clients to cover account creation, software onboarding, and initial project setup costs.
  24. Offer a loyalty discount (e.g., 10% off next project) for repeat clients to incentivize long-term relationships and referrals.
  25. Price motion graphics for e-learning platforms higher if the project includes scripting, storyboarding, and interactive elements beyond animation.
  26. Include a clause in your contract that allows you to reuse generic elements (e.g., backgrounds, transitions) in future projects to maximize your assets.
  27. Charge a 15% rush fee for same-day or next-day delivery to cover the premium for immediate turnaround and potential overnight work.
  28. Use a time-tracking app (e.g., Toggl, Clockify) to log every minute spent on a project to justify your billing and improve future estimates.
  29. Offer a free 15-minute consultation to qualify leads and discuss project scope before sending a formal quote, saving time on unqualified inquiries.
  30. Price motion design for mobile apps separately from web projects, considering platform-specific motion guidelines and user experience requirements.
