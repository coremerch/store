Motion Designer?s Pricing Playbook: 30 Rules to Price Projects with Confidence and Profit

pricing cheat sheet for motion designers ? 30 rules.

  1. Always calculate your hourly rate by dividing your desired annual income by 2,080 (work hours/year) and add 30% for taxes and business costs.
  2. Use the ?Per Second? pricing model for video-based projects by multiplying the total seconds by your rate per second (e.g., $50 per second for 30 seconds = $1,500).
  3. For logo animations, charge between $200?$500 for simple concepts and $800?$2,500 for complex animated logos with multiple states.
  4. Bundle your motion design services into packages (e.g., Social Media Pack: 3 animated posts for $600) to increase average order value.
  5. Charge extra for revisions beyond the agreed-upon 2?3 rounds; set a clear fee like $75 per additional revision.
  6. Use the ?Per Layer? pricing method for After Effects projects; charge $25?$50 per layer depending on complexity and animation time.
  7. For explainer videos, price based on length and complexity: 60?90 seconds simple videos start at $1,500; advanced 2?3 minute videos start at $5,000.
  8. Offer a ?Rush Fee? of 20?50% for projects due in under 48 hours to compensate for accelerated work.
  9. Implement a 50% deposit before starting any project over $1,000 to secure commitment and cover initial costs.
  10. Use the ?Per Asset? model for UI/UX motion design (e.g., hover states, micro-interactions) at $30?$150 per asset.
  11. Charge by the scene for 3D motion projects; simple scenes start at $300, while complex, rendered scenes start at $1,200.
  12. For kinetic typography projects, price per character per second; standard rates are $2?$5 per character per second.
  13. Set a minimum project fee of $300 for any motion design work to ensure profitability and respect your time.
  14. Offer subscription-based pricing for ongoing motion design needs (e.g., $200/month for 2 animated social posts).
  15. Use the ?Per Minute? pricing model for longer-form content like sizzle reels or demo reels at $200?$500 per minute.
  16. For live-action + motion graphics hybrid projects, charge $150?$400 per hour for post-production and animation integration.
  17. Charge a retainer fee for clients who need consistent motion design work (e.g., $1,000/month for 5 animated posts).
  18. Price motion graphics for advertisements based on the platform: $1,000 for a 15-second Instagram ad, $2,000 for a 30-second YouTube ad.
  19. Use the ?Per Shot? model for video editing projects; charge $100?$300 per shot depending on complexity and effects.
  20. For UI motion prototypes, charge $50?$150 per interactive element or transition.
  21. Set a late fee of 1.5% per week for overdue invoices to encourage timely payments.
  22. Offer a ?First-Time Client Discount? of 10% to attract new clients, but exclude it from your standard pricing structure.
  23. For 2D character animation, price per second; simple walk cycles start at $100 per second, while complex lip-sync starts at $300 per second.
  24. Charge a ?Style Frame? fee separately; each style frame costs between $150?$500 depending on detail and revisions.
  25. Use the ?Per Transition? model for motion graphics templates; charge $20?$100 per transition effect.
  26. For interactive web animations, price per interaction; simple hover animations start at $150, while complex scroll-triggered animations start at $500.
  27. Set a ?Kill Fee? of 30?50% of the total project cost if the client cancels mid-project to cover sunk costs.
  28. For motion design for apps, price per screen; simple animations start at $200 per screen, while complex animations start at $800 per screen.
  29. Charge a ?Rush Revision Fee? of $100 per hour for last-minute changes requested within 24 hours of delivery.
  30. Use the ?Per Frame? model for frame-by-frame animation projects; charge $5?$20 per frame depending on detail and complexity.
