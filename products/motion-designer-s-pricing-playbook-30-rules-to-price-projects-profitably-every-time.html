Motion Designer's Pricing Playbook: 30 Rules to Price Projects Profitably Every Time

pricing cheat sheet for motion designers ? 29 rules.

  1. Always ask for the project scope upfront?never quote without knowing deliverables, revisions, and deadlines.
  2. Use a rate calculator that factors in your living costs, taxes, software subscriptions, and desired profit margin before quoting.
  3. Charge per second of final animation for explainer videos, but cap the rate at a fixed maximum to avoid scope creep.
  4. For social media motion graphics, offer tiered pricing based on video length (15s, 30s, 60s) with a 10% discount for bulk orders.
  5. Include a 50% deposit before starting any project to secure your time and cover initial costs.
  6. Never work on a project without a signed contract that outlines payment schedules, kill fees, and late payment penalties.
  7. Add a 15% rush fee for projects due in less than 48 hours to compensate for overtime and priority scheduling.
  8. For logo animations or short branding clips, use a flat fee model with a maximum of 3 revisions included.
  9. When working with agencies, charge 2x your standard rate if they require exclusive rights or usage beyond the agreed scope.
  10. Offer a retainer package (e.g., 10 hours/month) at a 20% discount for clients who commit to ongoing work.
  11. Never discount your rate for friends or family?offer a free limited version or refer them to a trusted colleague instead.
  12. For corporate presentations or event motion graphics, charge a premium for custom assets, fonts, and branded color palettes.
  13. Include a clause in your contract that allows you to increase the project fee by 10% if the client requests more than 3 major revisions.
  14. Use a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana) to track milestones and send automated invoices upon completion of each phase.
  15. For e-learning or tutorial animations, charge per minute of final content with a minimum fee (e.g., $200 for up to 1 minute).
  16. Always send a detailed invoice with clear line items (e.g., concept development, animation, revisions) to justify your pricing.
  17. Offer a 5% discount for clients who pay within 7 days, but enforce a 5% late fee for payments overdue by more than 14 days.
  18. For UGC (user-generated content) motion templates, price based on the platform (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube) and license type (personal vs. commercial).
  19. Never undervalue your work?if a client balks at your price, ask if they?re comparing apples to oranges (e.g., their budget vs. your expertise).
  20. For film or cinematic motion work, charge a day rate (e.g., $800?$1,500) plus additional fees for complex rigging or 3D elements.
  21. Include a 10?15% buffer in your quote to account for unexpected revisions or technical delays.
  22. For website or app motion design, charge per component (e.g., hero section, loading animation, micro-interactions) with a project total cap.
  23. Offer a free 30-minute consultation to qualify leads and ensure the project is a good fit before sending a formal quote.
  24. For product launch animations, charge a premium for high-concept storytelling and include a post-launch review session in your fee.
  25. Use a tiered pricing model for YouTube channel intros/outros (e.g., basic: $150, premium: $400) with add-ons like custom sound design.
  26. Never start working on a project without a signed creative brief that includes the client?s brand guidelines and reference materials.
  27. For live event motion graphics (e.g., trade shows, conferences), charge a flat day rate plus travel expenses if the event is outside your local area.
  28. Offer a loyalty program where clients earn a free 1-hour motion graphic after every 5 completed projects.
  29. Always follow up with clients 30 days after project completion to request testimonials or referrals?include a link to your pricing page in your email signature.
