  1. Research industry-standard rates for your location and experience level before quoting a price for any project.
  2. Use a tiered pricing structure (e.g., Basic, Standard, Premium) to cater to different client budgets and project scopes.
  3. Always ask for a detailed project brief to avoid scope creep and to provide an accurate quote.
  4. Break down your pricing into line items (e.g., research, sketches, revisions, final delivery) to justify costs and prevent disputes.
  5. Charge a 30-50% deposit before starting any project to secure your payment and commitment.
  6. Offer package deals (e.g., logo + business card + social media kit) to increase your average project value.

  12. Offer rush fees (e.g., +20%) for projects with tight deadlines to compensate for increased workload.

  18. Set a late fee (e.g., 1.5% monthly) to incentivize clients to pay on time and cover your administrative costs.

  23. Include travel expenses in your quote if the project requires in-person meetings or on-site work.
  24. Set a maximum number of revisions (e.g., 2) in your contract to manage client expectations and protect your time.
  25. Offer a loyalty discount (e.g., 5%) for repeat clients to encourage long-term relationships and referrals.
  26. Use a project management tool (e.g., Trello, Asana) to track deliverables and deadlines, ensuring clients see your efficiency.
  27. Charge a setup fee (e.g., $50) for new projects to cover administrative costs like creating contracts and setting up files.
