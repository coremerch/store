Agency Escape Kit: 40 Client-Brief Prompts to Land High-Paying Solo Clients Without Guesswork

client brief prompts for agency escapees going solo ? 40 prompts.

  1. List the three biggest pain points you solved for your last agency client and turn them into direct questions for your solo brief.
  2. Write a one-sentence problem statement that your ideal client whispers to themselves at night.
  3. Identify the exact KPI your client tracks to measure success after your design work ships.
  4. Ask the client to share their top competitor?s website URL and list three elements they want to outperform.
  5. Request a dropbox or Google Drive link to their entire brand style guide before day one.
  6. Require the client to define their target user in three demographic sentences or less.
  7. Ask for their current conversion rate and average session duration on their main landing page.
  8. Demand a list of three adjectives they want users to feel after seeing the new design.
  9. Force the client to send you a screenshot of their most hated competitor?s checkout flow.
  10. Have them email you their brand voice guidelines or a sample of their best-performing social post.
  11. Require a signed document outlining who owns the final files and any usage rights post-project.
  12. Ask for their internal launch timeline including soft launch, press embargo, and public release dates.
  13. Request a list of three stakeholders who must approve the design before it goes live.
  14. Demand a PDF of their current site map and note which pages are non-negotiable to redesign.
  15. Ask the client to send you their brand colors in HEX codes and Pantone references if applicable.
  16. Require their latest brand mood board or three reference images they love for design direction.
  17. Force them to confirm their budget range in a single number before scoping begins.
  18. Ask for their preferred project management tool and invite you to their workspace on day one.
  19. Request access to their Google Analytics account with read-only access for the duration of the project.
  20. Have them list three past design projects they regret and explain why they fell short.
  21. Demand a 15-minute recorded Loom walkthrough of their current UX pain points from their perspective.
  22. Require a signed NDA that covers proprietary design concepts before any brainstorming begins.
  23. Ask the client to send you their top three customer testimonials and one case study that proves ROI.
  24. Force them to define what success looks like one month after launch in measurable terms.
  25. Request a list of three industry conferences or events where the new design will debut publicly.
  26. Ask for their tone guidelines?should your copy be playful, corporate, or somewhere in between?
  27. Require a timeline of their last redesign flop including what went wrong and who was responsible.
  28. Demand a list of three designers or agencies they?ve worked with previously and why they parted ways.
  29. Ask the client to send you a screenshot of their email signature so you can match their brand fonts.
  30. Force them to commit to a weekly 15-minute sync call during the design phase to avoid scope creep.
  31. Request a PDF of their brand mission statement or elevator pitch to align visuals with their core message.
  32. Ask for their social media handles and top-performing post metrics to inform your design decisions.
  33. Require a list of three technical constraints (e.g., CMS limitations, browser support) upfront.
  34. Demand a screenshot of their most recent customer support ticket related to design or UX issues.
  35. Ask the client to send you their brand font files or a link to purchase the exact typefaces they use.
  36. Force them to define their ideal user journey from first touch to conversion in six steps or less.
  37. Request a list of three design trends they hate and three they secretly love?so you avoid clich?s.
  38. Ask for their pricing page URL and note three elements they want to test or improve with new designs.
  39. Require a signed statement of work that includes deliverables, timeline, milestones, and payment schedule.
  40. Force them to send you their annual marketing budget so you can align your scope to their resources.
