Client Brief Mastery: 40 High-Converting Prompts for Web Designers to Secure Perfect Projects Every Time

client brief prompts for web designers ? 40 prompts.

  1. Write a prompt asking the client to describe their business in three sentences or less, focusing on their unique value proposition and target audience.
  2. Ask the client to provide three primary goals they want the website to achieve (e.g., increase sales, generate leads, build brand awareness).
  3. Request the client to share three examples of websites they like and explain why each one resonates with them.
  4. Prompt the client to define their ideal customer persona, including demographics, interests, and pain points.
  5. Ask the client to list five key features or functionalities they need on their website (e.g., e-commerce, booking system, blog).
  6. Request the client to provide their brand?s color palette, including primary, secondary, and accent colors, or ask if they want you to suggest one.
  7. Prompt the client to share their brand?s typography preferences, including font types and styles for headings, body text, and buttons.
  8. Ask the client to describe their brand voice and tone (e.g., formal, friendly, playful, professional) and provide examples of how they communicate in their marketing materials.
  9. Request the client to provide their logo files in multiple formats (PNG, SVG, PDF) and specify if they have any brand guidelines for logo usage.
  10. Ask the client to list any existing content they have (e.g., copy, images, videos) that should be included on the website and where it should be placed.
  11. Prompt the client to describe their preferred website layout (e.g., single-page, multi-page, parallax scrolling) and provide examples of layouts they like.
  12. Ask the client to specify the number of pages they need on their website and outline the structure for each page (e.g., Home, About, Services, Contact).
  13. Request the client to provide any specific design elements they want included (e.g., animations, illustrations, custom icons, sliders).
  14. Ask the client to describe their preferred navigation style (e.g., hamburger menu, dropdown, sticky header) and where they want key navigation links placed.
  15. Prompt the client to share their budget range for the project and any timeline constraints they have for launch or milestones.
  16. Ask the client to provide their domain name and hosting details, or ask if they need recommendations for a domain registrar or hosting provider.
  17. Request the client to describe any third-party integrations they need on their website (e.g., payment gateways, CRM, email marketing tools).
  18. Ask the client to list any competitors they want to analyze to ensure their website stands out in their industry.
  19. Prompt the client to share their preferred call-to-action (CTA) strategy (e.g., buttons, pop-ups, forms) and where they want CTAs placed on the site.
  20. Ask the client to describe their current website (if applicable), including what they like and dislike about it and why they?re redesigning it.
  21. Request the client to provide any legal requirements for their website (e.g., privacy policy, cookie consent, accessibility compliance like WCAG).
  22. Ask the client to specify if they have any existing style guides, mood boards, or design assets they want the new website to align with.
  23. Prompt the client to describe their preferred mobile responsiveness strategy (e.g., adaptive design, fully responsive, mobile-first approach).
  24. Ask the client to list any specific SEO requirements they have for the website (e.g., keyword integration, meta descriptions, alt tags).
  25. Request the client to provide their preferred social media links and how they want these integrated into the website (e.g., icons in header, social feed on homepage).
  26. Ask the client to describe their preferred loading speed expectations and any performance optimizations they want implemented (e.g., image compression, lazy loading).
  27. Prompt the client to share any specific security requirements for their website (e.g., SSL certificate, secure payment processing, data encryption).
  28. Ask the client to provide their preferred content management system (CMS) (e.g., WordPress, Squarespace, Webflow) or if they?re open to recommendations.
  29. Request the client to describe their preferred email capture and lead generation strategies (e.g., pop-ups, landing pages, contact forms).
  30. Ask the client to list any specific animations or micro-interactions they want included (e.g., hover effects, scroll-triggered animations).
  31. Prompt the client to share their preferred color scheme for call-to-action buttons (e.g., contrasting colors for visibility, brand-aligned colors).
  32. Ask the client to describe their preferred typography hierarchy (e.g., H1 for main headings, H2 for subheadings, body text size and line height).
  33. Request the client to provide any specific accessibility features they want implemented (e.g., high-contrast mode, screen reader compatibility, keyboard navigation).
  34. Ask the client to describe their preferred favicon and how they want it designed (e.g., simplified logo, custom icon).
  35. Prompt the client to share their preferred 404 page design and any custom messaging they want included (e.g., humorous, brand-aligned).
  36. Ask the client to list any specific testimonial or review integration requirements (e.g., star ratings, client logos, video testimonials).
  37. Request the client to describe their preferred blog or news section layout and functionality (e.g., grid, masonry, featured posts).
  38. Ask the client to provide their preferred contact form layout and fields (e.g., name, email, phone, message, dropdown menu for inquiries).
  39. Prompt the client to share their preferred pricing or service page layout, including any features to highlight (e.g., comparison tables, feature lists).
  40. Ask the client to describe their preferred portfolio or gallery layout and how they want images or projects displayed (e.g., carousel, grid, lightbox).
