  3. Offer three pricing tiers for logo animations: Basic ($150-$300), Standard ($400-$800), and Premium ($1,000-$2,500), each with increasing deliverables.

  6. Set a minimum project fee of $500 for any motion design work under 10 hours to avoid undervaluing your time.
  7. Offer retainer packages for ongoing motion graphics work (e.g., $1,500-$3,000/month for 2-3 deliverables), providing steady income and client loyalty.

  9. Use a day-rate for complex projects like full-length animated shorts, ranging from $500-$1,500 per day depending on experience and studio size.
  10. Implement a rush fee of 20-50% for projects with a deadline of less than 48 hours to compensate for overtime and priority scheduling.

  16. Always provide a detailed quote breakdown in your proposal, listing hours, deliverables, and any add-ons to justify your pricing.
  17. Offer a discount of 5-10% for upfront payments (e.g., full payment before project start) to improve cash flow and reduce invoicing hassle.
  18. Charge a 15% late fee on overdue invoices to encourage timely payments without damaging client relationships.
  19. For YouTube intros/outros, set a price of $300-$1,000 based on length, style, and whether custom music is included.
  20. Use a subscription model for template-based motion graphics (e.g., $50-$150/month for unlimited access to your animated templates).
  21. Charge $400-$1,200 for a custom animated infographic, factoring in research, design, animation, and revisions.
  22. Set a 50% milestone payment for projects over $2,000, with the remaining 50% due upon final delivery.

  27. Use a project-based fee for title sequences in corporate videos, charging $800-$2,500 for a 30-60 second animated title sequence.
  28. Charge $150-$400 per animated social media story or post, with higher rates for branded content or multiple slides.
  29. For motion design audits or consultations, charge $100-$300 per hour to review client work and provide actionable feedback.
